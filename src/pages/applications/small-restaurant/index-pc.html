Common Pain Points for Small Restaurant Startups

The hardest part isn't opening — it's keeping the kitchen running smoothly, the flavor consistent, and the service fast.

Chefs Are Expensive and Hard to Retain

Experienced Southeast Asian chefs command high salaries — and the ones you train just leave. One sick day and the kitchen is paralyzed. Delivery orders pile up, dine-in customers wait, bad reviews follow.

Kitchen Too Small for Equipment

A 10–15 m² kitchen has to fit prep tables, stoves, and a packing area. Traditional gas ranges turn the whole kitchen into an oven in summer — staff can barely stand it.

New Chef Means New Flavor

Regulars come for your signature taste. Today Chef A cooks it, tomorrow Chef B — the regular takes one bite and asks "Did you change chefs?" That's how you lose customers.

Bottlenecked at Peak Service Speed

At 11:30, delivery and dine-in orders flood in simultaneously. Two woks can't keep up. Customers wait 20 minutes with no food — they cancel the order, and bad reviews arrive the next day.

Kitchen Like a Furnace, Monthly Bills Shocking?

Open-flame kitchens hit 40°C+ in summer. Staff can only endure 2 hours. Gas cylinders need replacing several times a month, electricity bills keep climbing — that's how profits get eaten.

Equipment Breaks = Business Stops, No One to Fix It?

At noon the stove breaks down. The next batch is prepped but can't cook. You call the supplier — they say 3–5 days. How do you serve for the next 3 days?

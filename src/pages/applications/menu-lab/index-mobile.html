Cross-border Flavors Hard to Replicate

Thai sour-spicy, Malay coconut-rich, Indonesian aromatic - each cuisine's heat control and seasoning differ. Traditional reliance on chef experience makes standardization difficult.

Long recipe development cycle

New dishes typically take 2-4 weeks from R&D to rollout, with high trial costs and slow response to market trends and seasonal menus.

High cost of multi-cuisine chefs

Covering multiple cuisines requires hiring chefs with different specialties, leading to high labor costs, especially for scarce Southeast Asian chefs overseas.

Chain store flavor deviation

Inconsistent flavors across locations and chefs make brand standardization difficult, affecting customer loyalty and repurchase rates.

Unstable ingredient supply chain

Authentic ingredients like Thai bird's eye chili, Malaysian torch ginger, and Indonesian candlenuts are hard to source and expensive to import. Seasonal shortages force menu removals, and local substitutes fall short in taste.

High turnover · Long training

Multi-cuisine kitchens require versatile chefs, but mastering Thai stir-fry, Malay stew, and Indonesian frying takes months. Newly trained chefs often get poached, creating endless training cycles.

Customer Story

In the bustling streets of Bangkok, where the steam from hotpot mingles with the aroma of spices, a local chain restaurant with 8 branches felt the growing pains of rapid expansion. Thai hotpot demands perfectly layered broth and balanced dipping sauces — traditionally dependent on each chef’s personal touch and experience. As daily output climbed to 1200 meals, management pressure multiplied: Bangkok’s F&B industry has an extremely high turnover rate, and core recipes fluctuated with every chef departure. New store training cycles were long, and inconsistent quality directly threatened the brand’s long-term reputation.

In the past, the kitchen was heavily dependent on manpower, requiring 24 employees to run smoothly. Every new store meant a long learning curve for new hires. The traditional "master-disciple" model was not only time-consuming but also became a shackle slowing down expansion. Facing increasingly fierce competition, management knew that relying solely on manual labor was no longer sustainable. Kitchen automation became the inevitable choice.

The turning point came with the introduction of YuKoLi’s commercial kitchen solution. The YuKoLi team went deep into each store’s kitchen, transforming the complex base sauce preparation and seasoning processes with intelligent automation. The smart cooking system took over the most labor-dependent cooking tasks, converting intuitive "wok hei" and seasoning ratios into precise digital parameters. The equipment also included modules tailored to Thai local taste preferences, locking the brand’s core recipes firmly in place. Kitchen staff transformed from sweating laborers into efficient system operators.

The results were immediate and substantial. After implementation, kitchen staff was reduced from 24 down to 12, cutting labor costs by 50% and saving the brand 270,000 THB monthly in cash flow. In Bangkok’s cutthroat F&B margins, this automation investment paid for itself in just 11.3 months. Beyond cost savings, the strategic value lay in achieving absolute standardization of the brand’s flavor profile.

Thanks to the system’s intelligent guidance, new staff training cycles were slashed by 75%. “New stores are fully operational by week 2 — it used to take at least 2 months,” the brand founder reflects. The biggest bottleneck to expansion wasn’t front-of-house traffic — it was the speed and stability of kitchen output. Today, every pot of broth tastes the same across all branches, finally breaking the chains that held back scalable growth.

When this Bangkok hotpot chain turned its kitchen into a digital operation, transforming the elusive "flavor" into a repeatable competitive advantage, they unlocked the key to market leadership. This story raises a question for every F&B operator still wrestling with high turnover and inconsistent quality: in a world of uncertainty, isn’t it time your kitchen leveled up?
